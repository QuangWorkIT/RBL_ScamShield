Vietnamese scam and phishing messages often evade detection through teencode, slang, and omitted diacritics, while conventional defenses suffer from high false-negative rates or induce severe alarm fatigue under solitary cloud detectors. We evaluate ScamShield-VN, an empirical two-tier architecture combining an on-device ViSoBERT screener with a three-agent forensic council for uncertain cases. Tier 1 fine-tunes ViSoBERT using Cost-Sensitive Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss ($\alpha = 5.0$) and ONNX INT8 quantization ($98.42\text{ MB}$, $70.7\text{ ms}$ median CPU latency). Across five independent training seeds, the mean validation scam recall was $95.64\%$ (SD $2.04$ percentage points) and mean Macro-F1 was $94.51\%$ (SD $0.96$ percentage points). On a locked frozen holdout of 267 messages ($\theta_{\text{scam}} = 0.90$), ViSoBERT attained $96.00\%$ scam recall ($72/75$; exact $95\%$ Clopper-Pearson CI: $[88.75\%, 99.17\%]$) and $4.17\%$ false-positive rate ($8/192$), significantly outperforming solitary Gemini 3.5 Flash in paired correctness ($p < 0.0001$). On a separate 117-message challenge set, a single-LLM baseline achieved higher raw scam recall ($97.96\%$) with two false alarms. The three-agent council incurred zero false alarms on 19 benign lures (exact $95\%$ Clopper-Pearson CI: $[0.00\%, 17.65\%]$) while abstaining on 10 edge cases. Relative to a five-agent panel, the three-agent council reduced API queries by $40.0\%$ but incurred a $5.10$ percentage point drop in scam recall (exact paired $p = 0.0625$). On an exploratory 384-message development set, the cascade resolved $96.6\%$ of traffic locally at $267.5\text{ ms}$ mean system latency and an estimated marginal API cost of $\$0.0153$ per 1,000 messages. These empirical findings support selective cloud referral, while false alarms and abstentions warrant verification on larger independent cohorts.
