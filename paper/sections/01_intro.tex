\section{Introduction}

Online scams caused an estimated VND 18.9 trillion (approximately US\$744 million) in losses in Vietnam in 2024, according to a National Cybersecurity Association survey reported by VietNamNet \cite{vietnamnet2024scams}. Smishing, or SMS phishing, uses fraudulent text messages containing malicious links to steal sensitive information or cause financial losses; research has also identified message variants and text-evasion attacks as challenges for detection \cite{seo2024ondevice}. In Vietnam, authorities have warned of fake traffic-fine notices delivered through calls or messages and fraudulent online job offers requiring upfront payments \cite{mst2024scams}. Beyond these deceptive tactics, Vietnamese language processing presents additional challenges. Vietnamese social media texts contain informal vocabulary, teencode, and missing diacritics, and experiments have shown that removing diacritics can reduce the performance of pre-trained language models \cite{nguyen2023visobert}. These findings motivate evaluating Vietnamese SMS detectors against both deceptive message content and non-standard linguistic forms.

Researchers have applied deep learning and large language models (LLMs) to detect threats in text messages \cite{saias2025advances}. For Vietnamese, PhoBERT provides a pre-trained encoder that has been evaluated on several NLP tasks \cite{nguyen2020phobert}. More directly related to phishing detection, MLTEA combines PhoBERT and XLM-RoBERTa to process Vietnamese--English social media text. The model outperforms monolingual and multilingual baselines on both clean text and inputs with structural obfuscation \cite{nguyenxuan2026mixed}. Other studies have examined whether generative LLMs can identify phishing through prompting. SecureNet compares GPT-4 and Gemini 1.5, using instruction and chain-of-thought prompting, with a fine-tuned DeBERTa V3 classifier. Its findings show that prompt-based LLMs can detect phishing content across the evaluated datasets \cite{mahendru2024securenet}.

However, the on-device and cloud-based studies reviewed here do not evaluate a cost-sensitive local--cloud cascade for Vietnamese SMS \cite{seo2024ondevice,mahendru2024securenet}. The missing evidence concerns when an uncertain local prediction should be referred to an LLM and how the routing threshold affects missed scams, false alarms, end-to-end latency, and API cost. This matters because accepting uncertain local predictions can leave scams undetected or flag legitimate messages, while sending every message to the cloud adds delay and operating costs. SecureNet reports LLM prediction latencies of 13--15~s per sample in its experimental setup \cite{mahendru2024securenet}. An empirical evaluation is therefore needed to determine whether selective cloud fallback improves scam recall while keeping false alarms, response time, and API expenditure within deployment requirements.

This paper evaluates Vietnamese language models and cloud LLMs for SMS scam detection and proposes a two-tier architecture. Our primary contributions are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multi-Seed Evaluation:} We establish a standardized benchmark on $2,665$ real-world Vietnamese SMS messages ($N=267$ isolated frozen test set) across 5 deterministic random seeds, comparing fine-tuned Vietnamese language models (PhoBERT-base, PhoBERT-large, FPT ViBERT) against few-shot cloud LLMs (Gemini 3.5 Flash).
    \item \textbf{Cost-Sensitive Fine-Tuning:} We fine-tune ViSoBERT~\cite{nguyen2023visobert} under a Cost-Sensitive Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{WBCE}}$, $\alpha=5.0$), demonstrating a dominant Scam Recall of $95.62\% \pm 2.25\%$ and Macro-F1 of $92.51\% \pm 2.39\%$.
    \item \textbf{Statistical and Pareto Profiling:} Through McNemar's paired tests and deployment profiling, we establish that ViSoBERT attains Pareto-optimality over large-scale encoders ($3.8\times$ smaller than PhoBERT-large) while operating at $\approx 15\text{ ms/message}$ on consumer CPU hardware.
    \item \textbf{Two-Tier Local--Cloud Architecture:} We propose a pipeline that first classifies SMS messages using a local model. The system returns the local prediction when its confidence meets a chosen threshold and routes the remaining messages to a cloud LLM for further analysis. This design reserves cloud API calls for uncertain cases.
\end{itemize}
