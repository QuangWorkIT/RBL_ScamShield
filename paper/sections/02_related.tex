\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}

To systematically contextualize our work within the evolution of telecommunications threat triage, we analyze peer-reviewed investigations organized thematically and chronologically across four dominant research thrusts.

\subsection{Theme 1: Classical Machine Learning and Rule-Based Filtering}
Early investigations into telecommunications spam filtering chronologically evaluated shallow machine learning algorithms, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naive Bayes, and decision tree ensembles paired with TF-IDF or lexical n-gram representations~\cite{tuan2023evaluating, xie2025comparing, ghourabi2023enhancing}. Saeed~\cite{saeed2023method} demonstrated strong baseline classification on standardized Latin corpora. However, as threat syndicates escalated evasion tactics, Xie~\cite{xie2025comparing} revealed that shallow classifiers degrade precipitously when confronted with out-of-vocabulary teencode, diacritic stripping, and orthographic substitutions prevalent in Vietnamese smishing. Furthermore, static carrier regex rules incur severe maintenance overhead and fail to capture conversational persuasion tactics.

\subsection{Theme 2: Pretrained Language Models for Low-Resource Text Classification}
The transition to transformer-based contextual representations revolutionized localized threat detection. For Vietnamese text, PhoBERT~\cite{nguyen2020phobert} established the benchmark for syllable-level encoding. Cam and Binh~\cite{cam2026vnsed} demonstrated $86.47\%$ accuracy applying PhoBERT to Vietnamese email spam, while Nguyen-Xuan et al.~\cite{nguyenxuan2026mixed} designed a dual-encoder reaching an F1-score of $0.940$ on code-mixed phishing lures. Nevertheless, existing Vietnamese language model applications almost universally rely on standard unweighted cross-entropy loss. Because deceptive lures represent an extreme minority in authentic traffic streams, unweighted training heavily biases decision thresholds toward the majority benign class, yielding dangerous false-negative rates on subtle, indirect fraud vectors.

\subsection{Theme 3: LLMs and Edge-Deployed Small Language Models}
Subsequent research explored generative Large Language Models (LLMs) and on-device Small Language Models (SLMs) for threat triage~\cite{saias2025advances, zhou2026fraudsmswalker, elzemity2026agentic}. Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2026fraudsmswalker} comprehensively benchmarked commercial frontier models (GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini) on SMS fraud, discovering that while fraud recall reached up to $90.13\%$, benign accuracy dropped to $12.81\%$ due to severe hyper-vigilance. To enable real-time execution, mobile architectures like QuishingShield~\cite{banda2026quishingshield} and distilled edge models~\cite{mrinal2026resilient, phap2026edgeai} achieved sub-$50\text{ ms}$ latencies on CPU hardware. However, these edge pipelines focus primarily on English text and omit asymmetric penalty functions essential for Vietnamese social media dynamics.

\subsection{Theme 4: Multi-Agent Consensus, Correlated Errors, and Role Specialization}
The latest paradigm in LLM-based reasoning involves multi-agent debate and ensemble jury architectures~\cite{chan2024chateval, du2024improving, chen2025multiphishguard}. In-context deliberative frameworks like ChatEval~\cite{chan2024chateval} and MultiPhishGuard~\cite{chen2025multiphishguard} demonstrated that assigning distinct personas enhances factual reasoning. However, recent theoretical work by Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025correlated} and Smit et al.~\cite{smit2024should} demonstrated that commercial LLMs exhibit correlated failure modes, frequently co-failing on identical deceptive patterns. As shown by Ladha~\cite{ladha1992condorcet}, majority voting mechanisms fail to guarantee accuracy when agent votes are correlated. Consequently, naive majority consensus cannot resolve false alarm fatigue without an explicit, adversarial role—such as a Public Defender—engineered to de-correlate errors and actively defend legitimate transactional patterns.
