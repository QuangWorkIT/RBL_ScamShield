\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}

To systematically contextualize our work within the evolution of telecommunications threat triage, we analyze peer-reviewed investigations organized across four foundational research thrusts: (i) classical machine learning and rule-based filtering under orthographic evasion; (ii) pretrained language models and cost-sensitive text classification; (iii) frontier large language models, cascaded deferral, and on-device edge deployments; and (iv) multi-agent deliberation, structured prompting, and correlated error mitigation.

\subsection{Theme 1: Classical Machine Learning and Rule-Based Filtering}
Early investigations into telecommunications spam detection systematically evaluated shallow machine learning algorithms, including Support Vector Machines (SVM), Naive Bayes, and decision tree ensembles paired with TF-IDF or lexical $n$-gram representations~\cite{saeed2023method, xie2025comparing, ghourabi2023enhancing}. While Saeed~\cite{saeed2023method} demonstrated competitive baseline classification accuracy on standardized, formal Latin corpora, Xie~\cite{xie2025comparing} revealed that shallow classifiers deteriorate substantially when exposed to informal text variations. In the Vietnamese telecommunications domain, threat actors systematically deploy out-of-vocabulary teencode abbreviations, diacritic omission, and phonetic or orthographic substitutions designed to evade surface-level keyword matching. Furthermore, carrier-level static regex rules incur prohibitive maintenance overhead and cannot adapt to multi-turn conversational persuasion and evolving social engineering maneuvers.

\subsection{Theme 2: Pretrained Language Models and Cost-Sensitive Text Classification}
The adoption of transformer-based contextual representations marked a notable advancement in localized threat detection. For Vietnamese text processing, PhoBERT~\cite{nguyen2020phobert} established an early benchmark. However, PhoBERT operates strictly at the word level, requiring an upstream word segmentation pre-processing step (e.g., RDRSegmenter). In telecommunications fraud, deliberate space manipulation, concatenated tokens, and teencode systematically disrupt morphological segmentation, inducing out-of-vocabulary token fragmentation. To mitigate segmentation brittleness on informal user-generated text, ViSoBERT~\cite{nguyen2023visobert} was pretrained on a 60~GB social media corpus using Byte-Pair Encoding (BPE) subword tokenization, preserving morphological integrity across noisy social colloquialisms.

Recent studies applied contextual encoders to localized threats: Tu\'{a}n et al.~\cite{tuan2023evaluating} evaluated PhoBERT and recurrent baselines on multi-accent SMS, Cam and Binh~\cite{cam2026vnsed} attained $86.47\%$ accuracy on Vietnamese spam email, and Nguyen-Xuan et al.~\cite{nguyenxuan2026mixed} proposed a dual-encoder architecture achieving an F1-score of $0.940$ on code-mixed phishing lures. Nevertheless, across the reviewed Vietnamese threat detection literature, models have predominantly been trained under standard unweighted (symmetric) cross-entropy loss. Grounded in the principles of cost-sensitive learning~\cite{elkan2001foundations, lin2017focal}, telecommunications threat filtering imposes deeply asymmetric operational error costs: a false positive causes transient inconvenience, whereas a false negative exposes mobile subscribers to credential theft and financial extortion. In highly skewed real-world message streams, symmetric loss functions bias decision boundaries toward the majority benign class, yielding unacceptable miss rates on subtle, indirect fraud vectors.

\subsection{Theme 3: Frontier LLMs, Cascaded Deferral, and On-Device Edge Deployments}
Subsequent research examined generative Large Language Models (LLMs) and on-device Small Language Models (SLMs) for threat triage~\cite{saias2025advances, zhou2026fraudsmswalker, elzemity2026agentic}. Zhou et al.~\cite{zhou2026fraudsmswalker} comprehensively benchmarked frontier commercial LLMs (including GPT-4o, Claude, and Gemini) on SMS fraud, revealing that while fraud recall ranged between $64.16\%$ and $90.13\%$, benign recall collapsed to $12.81\%\text{--}30.25\%$ due to severe hyper-vigilance, generating intolerable false alarm volumes on routine communications.

To resolve operational latency and computational overhead on edge mobile devices, lightweight on-device architectures have emerged. Banda et al.~\cite{banda2026quishingshield} deployed a distilled student model (derived from Llama-3.2-3B) for multi-modal QR phishing with a latency of $25.34\text{--}45\text{ ms}$, while Phap et al.~\cite{phap2026edgeai} implemented a lightweight BiGRU equipped with Weighted Binary Cross-Entropy (WBCE) on English SMS corpora, attaining an inference latency of $0.25\text{ ms}$ on CPU hardware. While Phap et al. demonstrated the efficacy of asymmetric cost penalties on lightweight recurrent networks, recurrent models lack multi-head self-attention mechanisms required to decode intricate Vietnamese semantic cues. Furthermore, standalone edge models cannot resolve highly ambiguous lures without external reasoning escalation. This necessitates an asymmetric model deferral and cascaded filtering architecture~\cite{chen2023frugalgpt, mozannar2020consistent}, complemented by selective classification with a reject/unknown option~\cite{chow1970optimum} to safely route ambiguous traffic.

\subsection{Theme 4: Multi-LLM Deliberation, Structured Prompting, and Correlated Error Mitigation}
The emergence of LLM-as-a-judge frameworks and multi-agent deliberation architectures has offered new mechanisms for complex reasoning under uncertainty~\cite{chan2024chateval, du2024improving, chen2025multiphishguard}. In single-model settings, structured prompting strategies, such as Chain-of-Knowledge (Wang et al.~\cite{wang2024boosting}), establish competitive zero-shot baseline performance. Simultaneously, multi-agent frameworks like ChatEval~\cite{chan2024chateval} and Panels of LLM evaluators (PoLL, Verga et al.~\cite{verga2024poll}) demonstrate that ensembling diverse model outputs improves reasoning robustness over solitary evaluators.

However, theoretical analyses by Ladha~\cite{ladha1992condorcet} established that the asymptotic error convergence guaranteed by the Condorcet Jury Theorem degrades substantially when individual voter judgments exhibit positive correlation. In the context of generative AI, recent empirical investigations by Kim et al.~\cite{kim2025correlated} and Smit et al.~\cite{smit2024should} demonstrated that frontier LLMs frequently display correlated failure modes, simultaneously co-failing on deceptive boundary patterns. Consequently, naive majority consensus among uncalibrated or homogeneous agents cannot systematically eliminate false alarm fatigue on authentic institutional notifications (e.g., banking OTPs with urgent verbiage). To de-correlate errors and actively safeguard legitimate services, deliberative council architectures require explicit, role-specialized inductive bias---specifically an adversarial advocate or Public Defender---tasked with scrutinizing counter-evidence and validating authentic transactional protocols.

\subsection{Synthesis and Identified Research Gaps}
Table~\ref{tab:related_work} synthesizes the comparative landscape. Across the surveyed literature, four fundamental gaps emerge:
(i)~\textbf{GAP~1 (Linguistic \& Tokenization Mismatch):} Prior Vietnamese language models rely on word segmenters vulnerable to teencode obfuscation, whereas robust subword tokenizers have not been tailored to Vietnamese smishing triage;
(ii)~\textbf{GAP~2 (Symmetric Loss Misalignment):} Existing Vietnamese transformer architectures are optimized under symmetric loss, failing to reflect asymmetric telecommunication fraud damage;
(iii)~\textbf{GAP~3 (Edge-Cloud Execution Dilemma):} Prior studies bifurcate into resource-heavy cloud LLMs that suffer from hyper-vigilance and latency bottlenecks, or rigid edge models lacking escalation capabilities for ambiguous lures;
(iv)~\textbf{GAP~4 (Correlated Consensus Failure):} Multi-LLM ensembles lack adversarial counter-balancing mechanisms, resulting in correlated false alarms on urgent benign alerts.
These four gaps directly motivate the two-tier, cost-sensitive, and Public Defender-governed architecture of ScamShield-VN detailed in Section~\ref{sec:methods}.
