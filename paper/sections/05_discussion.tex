\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This section interprets our empirical findings, deconstructs the systemic behavioral failure modes of commercial cloud LLMs, examines the scientific rationale for our multi-agent council upgrade, and discusses trade-offs between parameter scaling and loss formulation.

\subsection{Pareto-Optimality of Cost-Sensitive Social Encoders}
The empirical benchmarks establish that fine-tuning ViSoBERT under a cost-sensitive objective function ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{WBCE}}$, $\alpha=5.0$) achieves an optimal Pareto frontier for mobile edge threat screening. By applying a 5-fold steeper gradient penalty on False Negatives during backpropagation, the decision boundary shifts to capture subtle orthographic obfuscations and non-standard teencode variants that symmetric baselines disregard.

Crucially, ViSoBERT attained an elevated Scam Recall of $95.62\% \pm 2.25\%$ without suffering from precision collapse ($89.79\% \pm 5.15\%$). Qualitative inspection of the 12 discordantly detected test instances where ViSoBERT correctly identified scams missed by PhoBERT-base revealed that $9$ cases ($75\%$) incorporated colloquial Vietnamese internet slang, deliberate letter substitutions (e.g., \textit{``chuyen tien nhanh'', ``nhan qua lien tay'', ``0 dong''}), or non-diacritic text. This resilience stems directly from ViSoBERT's domain-specific pre-training on Vietnamese social media comments, providing robust sub-word tokenization and contextual representations for informal digital communications.

\subsection{Deconstructing the Single-LLM Alarm Fatigue Phenomenon}
A primary empirical insight of this work is the breakdown in classification behavior observed in cloud-based Large Language Models. While Gemini 3.5 Flash achieved an unblemished Scam Recall of $100.00\% \pm 0.00\%$, its Precision collapsed to $64.47\% \pm 1.52\%$, yielding a severe \textbf{False Alarm Rate (FAR)} of:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:false_alarm_rate}
\text{FAR} = 100\% - \text{Precision} = 100\% - 64.47\% = 35.53\%
\end{equation}
In effect, over one in every three legitimate messages evaluated by the prompt-engineered LLM was mistakenly flagged as fraudulent.

Error analysis reveals two systemic root causes for this degradation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hyper-Vigilant Urgency Bias:} In-context learning prompts instructing the LLM to maximize fraud detection induce extreme hyper-vigilance. The model systematically penalizes legitimate transactional notifications—such as automated One-Time Passwords (OTPs) from commercial banks, delivery confirmation alerts, and official telecom promotional SMS—solely due to the presence of urgency markers (e.g., \textit{``xac thuc ngay'', ``ma OTP quy khach''}) or embedded short URLs.
    \item \textbf{Contextual Over-Generalization:} Standard zero-shot and few-shot prompts lack calibrated probability outputs, forcing the model to issue binary verdicts based on surface-level threat heuristics rather than fine-grained statistical likelihoods.
\end{enumerate}

From a human-factors security perspective, a $35.53\%$ false alarm rate precipitates severe \textbf{alarm fatigue}. When legitimate daily banking and carrier notifications are repeatedly blocked or flagged as high-risk, users experience cognitive burden and quickly lose trust in the defensive platform, ultimately disabling safety controls. Furthermore, Gemini 3.5 Flash incurred an average network API latency of $4,912\text{ ms}$ per message ($4.91\text{ s}$), rendering standalone cloud LLMs fundamentally unviable for sub-second, offline on-device screening.

\subsection{Scientific Motivation for Amendment v1.1: 5-Agent Forensic Council}
The empirical breakdown of single-LLM precision provides the direct scientific rationale for our pre-registered proposal amendment (Amendment v1.1). Rather than deploying cloud LLMs as solitary binary screeners, they are re-architected into a specialized \textbf{5-Agent Multi-LLM Forensic Council} invoked exclusively when Tier 1 edge confidence falls below $\tau = 0.90$.

To suppress single-agent hyper-vigilance, the council introduces an explicit \textbf{Public Defender / Devil's Advocate agent}~\cite{du2024improving, chen2025multiphishguard}. Tasked specifically with discovering legitimate sender brand markers, verifying SMS Brandname protocols, and arguing for benign interpretations, the Public Defender acts as an adversarial counter-weight to threat-seeking agents. This multi-agent deliberation restores precision on ambiguous transactional samples, effectively neutralizing alarm fatigue while maintaining high detection sensitivity.

\subsection{Failure of Parameter Scaling Without Asymmetric Loss}
Our experimental comparison between PhoBERT-base-v2 ($135\text{M}$ parameters) and PhoBERT-large ($370\text{M}$ parameters) demonstrates that parameter scaling cannot overcome a symmetric loss formulation. Despite possessing nearly $3\times$ more parameters, requiring $3.45\times$ longer training time ($1,457.7\text{ s}$ vs. $422.2\text{ s}$), and occupying $1.48\text{ GB}$ of memory ($3.79\times$ larger), PhoBERT-large attained a Scam Recall of only $92.33\% \pm 3.44\%$, missing more active scam lures than the 97M/110M ViSoBERT encoder ($95.62\% \pm 2.25\%$).

This finding confirms a key scientific principle for low-resource safety-critical NLP: in imbalanced threat detection tasks, the mathematical design of the cost-sensitive loss function ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{WBCE}}$) and domain-matched pre-training exercise a far greater influence on task performance than sheer model parameter scale.
