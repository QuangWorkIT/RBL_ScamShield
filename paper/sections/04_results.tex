\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the empirical performance metrics, computational benchmarks, and paired statistical hypothesis testing conducted on the frozen evaluation dataset.

\subsection{Research Question Evaluation and Descriptive Metrics}
Our primary research question investigated whether fine-tuning ViSoBERT under a cost-sensitive objective function yielded superior Scam Recall compared to standard transformer baselines and few-shot cloud LLMs on the isolated partition of $N = 267$ messages. The hypotheses were formulated as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_0$ (Null Hypothesis):} There is no statistically significant difference in paired classification performance between the proposed ViSoBERT + WBCE ($\alpha=5.0$) model and comparative baselines on the frozen test partition ($p \ge 0.05$).
    \item \textbf{$H_1$ (Alternative Hypothesis):} The paired classification performance differs significantly between the proposed ViSoBERT model and comparative baselines ($p < 0.05$).
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_summary} presents the empirical results aggregated over $K = 5$ independent deterministic random seeds ($\text{SEEDS} = [42, 100, 123, 999, 2026]$) for the deep learning models, alongside the deterministic few-shot Gemini 3.5 Flash baseline evaluated at a temperature of 0.0. Across all $267$ test samples and $5$ experimental runs, the models generated exactly $0$ invalid responses ($0.0\%$ invalid rate).

\subsection{Paired Statistical Significance Testing}
Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar} presents the paired two-tailed McNemar test statistics evaluated across the $N=267$ test partition, where $b$ represents the count of samples correctly classified exclusively by ViSoBERT, and $c$ denotes samples correctly classified exclusively by the comparative baseline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Cloud LLM (Gemini 3.5 Flash):} ViSoBERT correctly classified $b = 38$ instances that Gemini misclassified, whereas Gemini was uniquely correct on only $c = 2$ instances. The resulting continuity-corrected test statistic was $\chi^2 = 30.625$, yielding an exact two-tailed $p$-value of $p < 0.0001$ and an Odds Ratio of $\text{OR} = 19.00$. Consequently, the null hypothesis $H_0$ was decisively rejected, confirming a statistically significant difference in performance.
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Transformer Baselines:} In pairwise comparisons against PhoBERT-base-v2 ($b=12, c=4, p=0.0768$), FPT ViBERT-base ($b=14, c=5, p=0.0636$), and PhoBERT-large ($b=9, c=3, p=0.1460$), the exact $p$-values exceeded the significance threshold of $\alpha = 0.05$. Therefore, $H_0$ could not be rejected based solely on overall paired binary classification accuracy. Nevertheless, across all three transformer baselines, ViSoBERT consistently maintained a positive discordance, demonstrating an Odds Ratio ranging from $2.80$ to $3.00$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Inference Latency and Resource Utilization}
Computational profiling highlighted substantial divergences in operational efficiency among the evaluated models:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ViSoBERT ONNX INT8:} The proposed model achieved a CPU execution latency of $15.8\text{ ms/message}$. The cumulative training process across all $5$ independent runs required $422.2\text{ seconds}$, and the deployed model occupied a minimal storage footprint of $390\text{ MB}$.
    \item \textbf{PhoBERT-large:} The baseline required $1,457.7\text{ seconds}$ of cumulative training time (approximately $3.45\times$ slower than ViSoBERT) and occupied $1,480\text{ MB}$ of storage ($3.79\times$ larger).
    \item \textbf{Gemini 3.5 Flash API:} The cloud-based LLM incurred an average network response latency of $4,912\text{ ms/message}$, which was approximately $326\times$ slower than ViSoBERT executing locally on a CPU. This latency accumulated to $6,537.0\text{ seconds}$ for full-set inference across all $5$ experimental runs.
\end{itemize}
