\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section strictly reports empirical performance metrics, computational benchmarks, and paired statistical hypothesis testing on the frozen evaluation benchmark.

\subsection{Research Question Evaluation and Descriptive Metrics}
Our primary research question investigates whether fine-tuning ViSoBERT under a cost-sensitive objective function yields superior Scam Recall compared to standard transformer baselines and few-shot cloud LLMs on the isolated $N = 267$ message partition:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$H_0$ (Null Hypothesis):} There is no statistically significant difference in paired classification performance between the proposed ViSoBERT + WBCE ($\alpha=5.0$) model and comparative baselines on the frozen test partition ($p \ge 0.05$).
    \item \textbf{$H_1$ (Alternative Hypothesis):} The paired classification performance differs significantly between the proposed ViSoBERT model and comparative baselines ($p < 0.05$).
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_summary} presents the empirical results aggregated over $K = 5$ independent deterministic random seeds ($\text{SEEDS} = [42, 100, 123, 999, 2026]$) for the deep learning models, alongside the deterministic few-shot Gemini 3.5 Flash baseline (temperature = 0.0). Across all $267$ test samples and $5$ experimental runs, exactly $0$ invalid responses were observed ($0.0\%$ invalid rate).

\subsection{Paired Statistical Significance Testing}
Table~\ref{tab:mcnemar} reports the paired two-tailed McNemar test statistics across the $N=267$ test partition, where $b$ represents the count of samples correctly classified exclusively by ViSoBERT, and $c$ denotes samples correctly classified exclusively by the respective baseline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Cloud LLM (Gemini 3.5 Flash):} ViSoBERT was uniquely correct on $b = 38$ instances, whereas Gemini was uniquely correct on $c = 2$ instances. The resulting continuity-corrected test statistic was $\chi^2 = 30.625$ with an exact two-tailed $p$-value of $p = 0.0000 < 0.001$ and an Odds Ratio of $\text{OR} = 19.00$. Consequently, the null hypothesis $H_0$ is decisively rejected.
    \item \textbf{Comparison with Transformer Baselines:} In pairwise comparisons against PhoBERT-base-v2 ($b=12, c=4, p=0.0768$), FPT ViBERT-base ($b=14, c=5, p=0.0636$), and PhoBERT-large ($b=9, c=3, p=0.1460$), the exact $p$-values exceeded the threshold $\alpha = 0.05$. Thus, $H_0$ cannot be rejected on overall paired binary accuracy alone. However, across all three comparisons, ViSoBERT consistently demonstrated an Odds Ratio of $2.80\text{--}3.00$ in positive discordance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Inference Latency and Resource Utilization}
Computational profiling indicates substantial operational divergence:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{ViSoBERT ONNX INT8:} Achieved a CPU execution latency of $15.8\text{ ms/message}$, requiring $422.2\text{ seconds}$ total fine-tuning across 4 epochs and occupying $390\text{ MB}$ of storage.
    \item \textbf{PhoBERT-large:} Required $1,457.7\text{ seconds}$ training time ($3.45\times$ slower) and occupies $1,480\text{ MB}$ ($3.79\times$ larger).
    \item \textbf{Gemini 3.5 Flash API:} Incurred an average network response latency of $4,912\text{ ms/message}$ ($326\times$ slower than ViSoBERT on CPU), totaling $6,537.0\text{ seconds}$ for full-set inference.
\end{itemize}
