\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

In alignment with our pre-registered research protocol, we explicitly analyze potential threats to validity and the concrete mitigation measures implemented.

\subsection{Internal Validity}
Internal experimental factors can affect whether observed performance differences can be attributed to the proposed approach. Random weight initialization and variation in training convergence can lead to different results across runs. To account for this variation, we evaluated the neural models using $K = 5$ fixed random seeds ($\text{SEEDS} = [42, 100, 123, 999, 2026]$) and reported the mean and sample standard deviation for each metric. For the cloud LLM baseline, we set the temperature to 0.0 to reduce sampling variability.

\subsection{External Validity}
Our benchmark focuses on Vietnamese mobile text messages and scam scenarios. The findings may not generalize to other languages or communication channels, such as enterprise email, where language use and scam tactics may differ. We therefore limit our empirical claims to the Vietnamese messages evaluated in this study. Although the two-tier routing design could be adapted to other languages and channels, its effectiveness in those settings requires separate evaluation.

\subsection{Construct Validity}
Overlap between training and test messages, along with errors in ground-truth labels, can bias the evaluation. We removed duplicate messages before splitting the corpus and reserved a frozen test set of $N = 267$ samples before hyperparameter tuning. Two annotators independently labeled the messages, with inter-annotator agreement of Cohen’s $\kappa = 0.88$. However, near-duplicate message templates and annotation errors may remain, and these measures do not rule out overlap between the test messages and the models’ pre-training data. 

\subsection{Conclusion Validity}
Small performance differences may be difficult to detect on our frozen test set of $N = 267$ messages, particularly for scam recall, which is calculated from only 75 scam messages. We compared paired classification outcomes using two-sided McNemar tests with continuity correction and reported the resulting $p$-values. These tests assess differences in overall classification accuracy and do not directly establish differences in scam recall. Small recall differences may therefore remain undetected, and non-significant results should not be interpreted as evidence that the models perform equivalently.
